# AACPS Approved Strengths and Needs — Source Examples and Writing Rules

## Purpose
These are therapist-supplied AACPS-approved examples of the *type of language* used for Section 9. The examples represent different students and are NOT factual data for any new student. Use only documented student-specific facts. Combine, adapt, and supplement wording with all additional information the therapist supplies. Do not copy contradictory observations, scores, classifications, grade, hand dominance, or gender from the examples.

## Family-readable report format
Use the exact two headings **Strengths as they relate to classroom performance** and **Needs as they relate to classroom performance**. Write readable, cohesive paragraphs grouped by functional themes, with clear transitions and concrete examples of classroom impact. Avoid dense run-on text, repetitive details, unexplained jargon, excessive bullets, raw HTML tags, and claims beyond the source evidence. Preserve precise performance data where verified. Distinguish teacher report from direct OT observation. Summarize standardized sensory findings only when the specific questionnaire and scores are confirmed. The example text contains contradictory sensory ratings because it describes different students; NEVER merge them. Do not invent missing XXXXX data or infer a score from behavior.
