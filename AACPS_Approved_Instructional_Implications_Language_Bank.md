# AACPS Instructional Implications — Public Statement Bank and Selection Rules

## Source and use
The statements below were repasted by the therapist from the AACPS PowerSchool public statement options on November 1, 2026. Preserve them as approved-style source wording; the therapist may edit, add, remove, or personalize recommendations. This is a **multi-select** category, unlike Section 10 Data Analysis, which is exactly one of four options.

## Required behavior for Section 12
- Retain the PowerSchool prompt in the complete preview only, not the copy-ready field: “Discuss the student's performance and instructional implications for participation in the general curriculum, or, for a preschool student, participation in appropriate activities.”
- Choose only relevant statements grounded in the student's actual functional performance, strengths, needs, and assessment findings. Do not dump the entire bank.
- Combine closely overlapping suggestions where appropriate, retaining practical detail and meaning. Add therapist-supplied strategies and allow the therapist to edit all choices.
- Start with the individualized introductory sentence below; follow with a concise, single-spaced bulleted list (•). Use family-readable wording and concrete classroom examples; do not use raw HTML.
- Never leave placeholders, conflicting pronouns, unverified assumptions, or the unfinished source fragment “Visual supports such as:” as a standalone bullet.
- Treat all student-specific claims as contingent on actual evidence; do not infer a need for sensory equipment, modified paper, breaks, or alternate seating from diagnosis alone.

## PowerSchool approved public statement options (source wording)

{FirstName} may benefit from the following modifications/accommodations in {.his,her} school environment to promote success and independence:

• After verbal, written, or demonstrated instructions, follow up with {FirstName} to check for understanding with the option for {FirstName} to paraphrase/repeat the instructions if needed.
• Consider implementing strategies (such as finger spaces, block spaces, spacing paper, etc.) to promote spacing between words and increase the readability of {FirstName}’s written work.
• Allow natural opportunities for movement in {FirstName}'s daily school routine. Consider engaging {FirstName} in classroom roles/tasks as appropriate, such as handing out books, transporting items, retrieving/putting away supplies, etc. to incorporate movement breaks into {.his,her} classroom environment. Consider scheduling additional opportunities for movement breaks outside the classroom (in the motor room).
• Consider exploring alternate seating options (such as wiggle cushion, wobble stool, etc.) that allow for some minimal movement when {FirstName} is sitting at {.his,her} desk.
• Consider exploring the use of sensory fidgets in the classroom setting as appropriate with student and staff education regarding the proper use of sensory fidgets.
• Provide {FirstName} with verbal and/or visual warnings for changes in routine when possible.
• Teaching unfamiliar skills through demonstration and additional assistance from the teacher/classroom staff should be available.
• Provide lines on all papers to orient {FirstName} to the designated writing space. Consider triple-lined paper (including a top line, dashed mid line, and baseline) to support differentiation in letter sizes (ascending letters, descending letters, uppercase letters, and lowercase letters).
• Allow the use of a letter/number strip to serve as a visual model for proper letter and number formations.
• Provide bold lines, margins, etc. for increased visual boundaries when {FirstName} is completing writing tasks.
• Allow {FirstName} to use modified paper, such as bold lined paper, block spaces, writing on every other line, spacing paper, grid paper, or paper with raised lines in order to improve overall readability of {.his,her} written work.
• Consider allowing additional opportunities for writing practice and/or independent work time related to building {FirstName}'s letter formation skills.
• Allow {FirstName} to use a pencil grip or modified writing tool to support a functional pencil grasp when writing.
• Visual supports such as:
• Adult redirection and assistance with formation errors with numbers and letters
• Due to difficulty with motor planning/learning new tasks, {FirstName} may benefit from visual supports and demonstration of new skills, paired with verbal directions.
• Efforts should be made to encourage naturally occurring implementation of sensory based strategies within the school environment in order to minimize disruption of classroom routines and instruction.
• Consider exploring the use of noise-reduction headphones as needed to diminish background noise or decrease the noise volume and intensity.
• Consider allowing {FirstName} to complete structured work tasks in a private space with adult support and limited visual distractions.

All options are selectable, not universal. Research support varies by intervention; do not imply the public bank itself establishes effectiveness.
