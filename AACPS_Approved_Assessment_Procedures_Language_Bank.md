## Battelle Developmental Inventory, third edition (BDI-3)

The Battelle Developmental Inventory is a standardized, individually administered assessment of key developmental skills in children from birth to 7 and is an excellent, generalized developmental test as it can be administered to both typical and non-typical developing children. There are five delineated purposes for use of the BDI-3 including assessment of typically developing children, assessment and identification of children with disabilities or developmental delays, planning and providing instruction and intervention, monitoring progress, and evaluation of programs servicing children. The BDI-3 assesses 5 domains including Adaptive, Cognitive, Communication, Motor (Fine, Gross, Perceptual) and Social-Emotional. Each domain reports scaled scores, percentile ranks and age equivalencies. The BDI-3 yields a Developmental Quotient (DQ) for each developmental domain and an overall BDI-3 Total DQ score that represents the child’s development across all five developmental domains. A DQ score is a standard score that has a mean of 100 and a standard deviation of 15.

## Bruininks-Oseretsky Test of Motor Proficiency (BOT-2)

This test measures various aspects of gross and fine motor development with eight subtests within four TESTING categories, some of which are timed. The test provides standard, percentile and scaled scores for the four testing categories. The four categories are: Fine Manual Control, Manual Coordination, Body Coordination and Strength and Agility which provide a total motor composite score, as well as a fine motor composite score. Standard scores for the composites are based on average score of 50 with 40-60 as the normal range. Age based standardized scores, percentile ranks and stanines can be determined for each subtest. Age equivalency is calculated for the complete composite battery only. Standard scores are available for students ages 4.0 to 21.0

## Beery-Buktenica Developmental Test of Visual-Motor Integration, 6th edition (BEERY VMI)

The Beery has a mean standard score of 100 with a standard deviation of 15; however, standard scores between 85 and 115 are considered average. Standard scores are available for ages 2-18.The Beery-Buktenica Developmental Test of Visual-Motor Integration-Sixth Edition (Beery VMI; Beery & Beery, 2010) is a norm-referenced test used to assess the ability to integrate visual and motor abilities in children age 2 to 18 years. It requires students to copy a sequence of geometric forms using paper and pencil. Two supplemental tests are available to assess visual and motor skills separately: the Beery VMI Visual Perception Test and the Beery VMI Motor Coordination Test.

## The Developmental Assessment for Young Children- second edition (DAYC-2)

The Developmental Assessment for Young Children is a developmental assessment for children from birth through 5-11 with possible delays in the following domains: cognition, communication, social-emotional development, physical development, and adaptive behavior. The area of physical development looks at both gross motor and fine motor skills. The domains can be assessed independently, and information about a child's abilities can be obtained through observation, interview of caregivers, and direct assessment. Each domain reports standard scores, percentile ranks and age equivalencies, as well as descriptive terms relating to the standard score ranges, including very poor (<70), poor (70-79) below average (80-89), average (90-110), above average (111-120), superior (121-130) and very superior (>130).

## Developmental Test of Visual Perception (DTVP-3)

This test measures visual perception with and without motor responses. Its five subtests make up composite measures of general visual perception, motor-reduced visual perception, and visual-motor integration. It provides Composite Index and percentile scores comparing the student with his or her age group, and age equivalents. Scaled Scores are obtained for subtests and are based on an average of 10 with a normal range of 7-13. For composites scores 100 is average with a normal range of 85-115. *Scores are available for students ages 4 through 12 years.

## Evaluation Tool of Children’s Handwriting (ETCH)

The ETCH evaluates the manuscript and cursive handwriting skill of students in grades 1-6. It assesses handwriting speed and legibility in writing tasks similar to those required of students in the classroom. ETCH tasks include alphabet and numerical writing, neat-far point copying, dictation and sentence generation. In addition, it assesses legibility components, pencil grasp, hand preference, manipulative skills with the writing tool and classroom observations. Writing speed is measure in letters per minute and percentages of numeral, letter and word legibility. Each writing task is scored and a combined score for all tasks is reported. It should be utilized only with students who have had a minimum of 10-12 weeks of experience with manuscript or cursive writing.

## Jordan Left-Right Reversal Test 3rd Edition

The Jordan Left-Right Reversals Test (3rd Edition) is an assessment of how well students (ages 5 through 18 years) can identify reversed images, letters, and numbers (in isolation and within text); and sequences of letters. The Jordan-3 is comprised of two parts and five subtests; the subtests tasks are presented in developmental sequence. The goal of each task is for the student to identify reversals of objects, letters, numbers, words, or letter sequences. Children ages 5 through 8 years of age are administered only Subtests 1A and 1B; children ages 9 through 18 are administered all subtests. Accuracy scores reflect the reversals correctly identified in each subtest. Error scores reflect reversals that were not identified and non-reversed letters, numbers, word, or sequences that were incorrectly identified as reversals. Percentile ranks, obtained for Accuracy and Error scores, are interpreted based on the frequency of scores in the normative sample. Scores at or above the 25th percentile fall within the average range; scores between the 24th and 11th percentiles fall within the borderline range; scores at or below the 10th percentile fall within the atypical range.

## Miller Function and Participation Scales (M-FUN)

The Miller Function and Participation Scales (M-FUN) is a developmental assessment tool that can be used to determine if a child has a developmental delay in the functional motor abilities needed to participate in early school years. M-FUN was developed for children ages 2 years 6 months through 7 years 11 months. Children taking the test engage in tasks that sample functional preschool and school fine motor activities (e.g. drawing, writing, and cutting with scissors) and gross motor activities (e.g., catching a ball, balancing, and jumping). Raw scores are converted into scaled scores and percentiles. A scaled score of 10 corresponds to the typical performance level of a given age group, and scaled scores between 7 and 13 are considered the range of typical performance.

## Motor-Free Visual Perception Test, fourth edition (MVPT-4)

The MVPT-4 is an individually administered assessment of visual-perceptual skills commonly used in everyday activities. This test assesses an individual’s visual perceptual ability with no motor involvement needed to make a response. The test format is a visual multiple choice: the individual is shown a drawing and is then asked to choose the matching drawing from a set of four or five on the following page. Five categories of visual perception are measured including spatial relationships, visual closure, visual discrimination, visual memory and figure ground. MVPT-4 norms are based on a stratified nationally representative sample of typically developing visual perception. The test provides both a perceptual quotient and a perceptual age score. Perceptual quotients have 100 as the average score with a standard deviation of 15, thus average perceptual quotients range from 85-115. MVPT-4 is quantified for ages 4 through 80 +.

## Peabody Developmental Motor Scales, Second Edition (PDMS-2)

This test is designed to evaluate children from birth through age 5 and is composed of six subtests that assess related motor abilities that develop early in life. These include reflexes, stationary (body control and equilibrium), locomotion, object manipulation, grasping and visual-motor integration. Results from these subtests are used to generate three composite scores: Gross Motor Quotient, Fine Motor Quotient and Total Motor Quotient. Scores are presented as percentiles, standard scores and age equivalents. Norms are based on a nationally representative sample of typically developing children and are stratified by age.

## Pediatric Evaluation of Disability Inventory (PEDI)

This test measures a child’s performance in several functional areas (self-care, mobility, and social function) and the amount of caregiver assistance and modifications required for that level of performance. It is administered by interviewing the caregiver. The test provides standard scores for Functional Skills and Caregiver Assistance in each area, comparing the child with his or her age group. Standard scores are based on an average of 50 with a normal range of 40-60 and are available for children aged 6 months to 7 ½ years.

## School Function Assessment (SFA)

This test measures a student’s performance of functional tasks that support participation in the academic and social aspects of the elementary school program (grades K-6). The test, which is completed by appropriate school staff, measures the student’s level of participation, the amount of assistance and modifications required for that participation, and performance in specific school activities. It then compares that information to criterion scores at either the grades K-3 level or the grades 4-6 level. A score of 100 means full grade-appropriate functioning in that area.

## Sensory Processing Measure - 2 (SPM-2)

The Sensory Processing Measure-2 is an integrated system of rating scales that enables assessments of sensory processing, praxis, and social participation. There are multiple questionnaires appropriate for specific age ranges including Preschool Children (2-5 years old), School Aged Children (5-12 years old) and Adolescents (12-21 years old). The assessment shows how sensory processing problems may manifest themselves in a variety of settings including school, home and the community. Raw scores are converted into T-Scores and Percentiles. When interpreting, the higher the score, the greater difficulty the student is experiencing. T-scores between 40-59 represent Typical Functioning, T-scores between 60-69 represent Moderate Difficulties and T-scores between 70-80 represent Severe Difficulties. Identified differences may or may not affect school performance.

## Sensory Profile 2

Toddler Sensory Profile 2-Caregiver Questionnaire is designed for children 7-35 months.
School Companion Profile 2 designed for students 3:0-14:11
Child Caregiver Questionnaire is designed for students 3:0-14:11
This family of assessments provides a standard method for documenting a child/student’s sensory patterns and supports the team in identifying the effect of sensory processing on functional performance in the home, school, or community.
The questionnaire lists a number of behaviors and asks the caregiver, teacher, or student to report the frequency of each behavior (Almost Always, Frequently, Half the Time, Occasionally, Almost Never, or Does Not Apply). The examiner is then able to tabulate and plot the Score Summary based on the rater responses. These scores provide a classification system to categorize a child's tendency for specific behaviors. The classification system consists of 5 categories that reflect specific groups of scores along the bell curve: Much Less Than Others, Less Than Others, Just Like the Majority of Others, More Than Others, and Much More Than Others. Scores one standard deviation or more from the mean are expressed as More or Less Than Others. Scores two standard deviations or more from the mean are expressed as Much More or Much Less Than Others.

## Sensory Symptoms Checklist –Gwen Wild

The purpose of this tool is to assist in developing an appropriate plan to address a student’s sensory needs which are impacting their access and/or participation in their educational program. This is NOT a diagnostic tool but rather an informal guide to be used as an indicator of over- or under- responsiveness.

## Test of Visual-Perceptual Skills (TVPS-4)

The TVPS is a series of tests which discern the student's ability to perceive, understand, and use visually presented materials. It is not a test of visual acuity. The TVPS-4 consists of a series of subtests measuring visual perception without motor responses. Scores are presented as individual subtest scaled scores and one overall standard score. Percentile ranks and age equivalents are also provided. Standard scores are available for students age 5 to 21-11 years.
