# AACPS-approved language examples — Factors Influencing Assessment Results

## Status and use
The four passages below were provided by an AACPS occupational therapist as examples of currently approved wording. They are **models, not universal factual statements**. For Section 8, combine relevant phrases into a coherent, student-specific narrative, add any therapist-supplied factors, and omit or adapt statements not substantiated for the student. Preserve clinical meaning. Do not assert a sensory checklist was completed, that a setting was used, that breaks were offered or requested, or that results were reliable unless documented and professionally supported. Do not invent interpreter use, cultural factors, examiner interactions, fatigue, environmental changes, or testing conditions. If validity cannot be established, do not state results are reliable. Keep PowerSchool's automatically generated question out of the copy-ready therapist-entry field.

## Approved example 1 — Natural learning environment and engagement
All structured observations/assessment procedures took place in {FirstName}'s natural learning environment (classroom, hallway transitions, and cafeteria). {FirstName} was very happy and willing to participate in teacher directed activities. {.He,She} sat beside {.his/her} peers on the carpet for whole group instruction and a movement break. {.He,She} was eager and willing to participate in all activities.

## Approved example 2 — Classroom routines and sensory profile
The examiner (occupational therapist) observed {FirstName} in {.his,her} daily natural environment (classroom setting). During the evaluation period, {FirstName} participated in familiar classroom routines. {FirstName}'s classroom teacher/special education teacher completed the sensory profile checklist as it relates to their observations of {FirstName}'s performance and participation in the classroom. Results should be considered as reliable and accurate for the purpose of this assessment with consideration to occupational therapy in the school environment.

## Approved example 3 — Controlled testing setting and requested breaks
All observations and assessment procedures were conducted in {FirstName}’s daily academic environment. Therapist directed activities measuring functional skills performance were conducted in a quiet, controlled room with limited environmental distractions in order to provide {FirstName} with the opportunity to work in a setting that would promote heightened focus/attention. {FirstName} requested occasional movement breaks and benefited from using an auditory timer to help with transitions back to seated work with the occupational therapist.

## Approved example 4 — Controlled setting, offered breaks, multiple sessions
All observations and assessment procedures were conducted in {FirstName}’s daily academic environment. Therapist directed activities measuring functional skills performance were conducted in a quiet, controlled room with limited environmental distractions in order to provide {FirstName} with the opportunity to work in a setting that would promote heightened focus/attention. {FirstName} was offered occasional movement breaks during the assessment period. Additionally, assessment procedures were split between multiple days to avoid mental fatigue and/or overload.

## Drafting rules
- Replace placeholders with verified name and pronouns; never leave template markers in the final report.
- Distinguish **requested** from **offered** movement breaks, and observed effects from presumed benefits.
- State classroom, hallway, cafeteria, quiet room, and number of sessions only when documented.
- Combine and streamline examples without copying conflicting circumstances.
- Incorporate therapist-provided observations, accommodations, attention, motivation, sensory/environmental factors, language or interpreter considerations, examiner interactions, and fatigue as applicable.
- Never conclude validity from cooperative participation alone; align any reliability statement with the therapist's actual validity judgment.
