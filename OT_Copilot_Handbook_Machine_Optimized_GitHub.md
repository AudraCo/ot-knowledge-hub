# AACPS Occupational Therapy Copilot Knowledge Base Handbook

### Disclaimer

This document is an unofficial resource developed by AACPS occupational therapy staff to support the use of Microsoft Copilot in school-based occupational therapy practice. It is intended as a reference tool only and does not replace AACPS procedures, professional clinical judgment, supervisory guidance, or district-approved documentation requirements.

## Purpose

Comprehensive AACPS OT evaluation writing and reporting guide built from AACPS-style evaluations.

## Master Report Structure

Purpose of Assessment; Socio-Cultural/Linguistic Considerations; Validity Statement; Background Information; Assessment Procedures; Test Scores; Assessment Results; Factors Influencing Assessment Results; Strengths and Needs; Data Analysis; Summary of Findings; Instructional Implications.

## Formatting Standards

Use <b>...</b> for headings that must remain bold in AACPS. Use bullet character •. Assessment Results follow Narrative → Lead-in → Bullets → Summary.

## Assessment Procedures Standards

Include approved descriptions for Beery VMI, Sensory Profile 2, SPM/SPM-2, M-FUN, PDMS-2 and other tools. Assessment descriptions may appear in Procedures and/or Results.

## Fine Motor Language Bank

Strengths: established hand dominance, pincer grasp, tripod grasp, bilateral coordination, paper stabilization. Needs: low tone, weak hand strength, immature grasp, reduced precision, manipulation challenges.

## Manipulation Skills Language Bank

Translation, shift, rotation, bilateral coordination, opening containers, bead stringing, coin manipulation, puzzle manipulation, tool use.

## Handwriting Language Bank

Strengths: readable letters, tracing, copying, left-to-right directionality. Needs: spacing, sizing, baseline orientation, pressure, reversals, letter formation, endurance.

## Visual-Motor Language Bank

Visual scanning, figure-ground, visual closure, copying, tracing, mazes, drawing, block design, visual discrimination.

## Sensory Profile 2 Language Bank

Seeking, Avoiding, Sensitivity, Registration, Auditory, Visual, Touch, Movement, Behavioral with definitions, observation examples, educational relevance. * denotes Much More Than Others.

## SPM-2 Language Bank

Vision, Hearing, Touch, Taste/Smell, Body Awareness, Balance and Motion, Planning and Ideas, Social Participation. Report score, interpretation, observations, educational impact.

## Functional Life Skills Bank

Dressing, toileting, feeding, arrival/dismissal, backpack management, locker management, hygiene, adaptive independence.

## Occupational Performance Bank

Participation, persistence, transitions, attention, preferred activities, emotional regulation, reinforcement preferences.

## Preschool / Child Find Bank

School readiness, play, sensory exploration, transitions, communication, prewriting, puzzles, adaptive skills.

## Autism / ACC Language Bank

Sensory regulation, movement seeking, self-injurious behavior, first/then supports, visual schedules, sensory diets, classroom participation.

## Factors Influencing Assessment Results

Document environment, participation, supports, validity, reliability, consistency of findings.

## Strengths and Needs Framework

Strengths = participation, motor strengths, learner characteristics. Needs = overview, motor, visual-motor, sensory, adaptive concerns.

## Data Analysis Framework

Supervisor-approved statement plus brief supporting findings. Avoid repeating Results.

## Summary of Findings Framework

Referral, key findings, strengths, educational significance, support needs.

## Instructional Implications Library

Visual schedules, first/then boards, slant boards, modified paper, movement breaks, heavy work, noise reduction, sensory supports, self-help practice.

## Goal Bank

Fine Motor, Manipulation, Cutting, Visual-Motor, Handwriting, Sensorimotor, Preschool Readiness, Functional Life Skills, Occupational Performance, Classroom Participation goals with baselines and objectives.

## Assessment-Specific Standards Implemented

Beery VMI, Sensory Profile 2, SPM, SPM-2, SSC-SV, M-FUN, PDMS-2. Placeholders remain for DTVP-3, TVPS-4, MVPT-4, ETCH, BOT-2, DAYC-2, BDI-3.

## Evaluation Archetypes

Fine Motor, Visual-Motor, Sensorimotor, Preschool/Child Find, Autism, ACC, High School Sensory/Regulation, Functional Life Skills, Occupational Performance, Reevaluation.

## Language Bank Samples

- Demonstrates established right-hand dominance.

- Demonstrates emerging visual scanning skills.

- Demonstrates difficulty integrating visual information with motor output.

- Benefits from visual models and repetition.

- Frequent movement breaks may improve regulation and participation.

- Student demonstrates weaknesses in the area of fine motor and visual-motor performance. Supports and modifications should be explored that will enable participation with classroom peers in the least restrictive environment.

### WHEN COMPLETING ASSESSMENT RESULTS

Organize Assessment Results according to the AACPS Occupational Therapy Functional Checklist using the following domains:

- Neuromuscular/Postural

- Manipulation Skills

- Response to Learning Environment

- Pencil Grasp and Handwriting

- Classroom Skills

- Visual / Visual-Perceptual Skills

- Occupational Performance and Functional Participation

Use narrative introductions, lead-in statements, and bullets. Maintain objective language and avoid interpretation.

### WHEN WRITING STRENGTHS AND NEEDS

Write Strengths and Needs in narrative format. Begin with:

"The following Strengths and Needs reflect the student's functional performance, participation, successes, and areas of difficulty within the educational environment."

Use <b>Strengths:</b> and <b>Needs:</b> headings.

Strengths should focus on participation, motor abilities, visual-motor abilities, and learner characteristics.

Needs should focus on the underlying skills that require development.

Do not place educational impact statements in the Needs section.

### WHEN WRITING DATA ANALYSIS

Keep Data Analysis brief. Begin with the AACPS-approved statement:

"The student demonstrates weaknesses in the area(s) of ________. Supports and modifications should be explored that will enable participation with classroom peers in the least restrictive environment."

Follow with one concise paragraph connecting assessment findings and classroom performance.

Do not rewrite the Assessment Results section.

### WHEN WRITING SUMMARY OF FINDINGS

Write Summary of Findings using the following structure:

Paragraph 1 – student, age, placement, and referral concern.

Paragraph 2 – fine motor/visual-motor/sensorimotor findings.

Paragraph 3 – primary areas of concern and standardized assessment findings.

Paragraph 4 – strengths.

Paragraph 5 – educational significance.

Summary should be comprehensive but concise and should stand alone if copied into other educational documents.

### WHEN WRITING INSTRUCTIONAL IMPLICATIONS

Write Instructional Implications using bullets only.

Do not use narrative paragraphs.

Include accommodations, environmental supports, classroom strategies, sensory supports, visual supports, movement supports, handwriting supports, functional life skill supports, or adaptive supports directly related to evaluation findings.

### WHEN WRITING GOALS

Write goals that are:

- Educationally relevant

- Directly tied to baseline data

- Observable

- Measurable

- Easy to progress monitor

- Focused on the underlying deficit rather than the symptom

Include:

- Annual Goal

- Three objectives

- Baseline

- Progress monitoring ideas

Do not use vague phrases such as "improve fine motor skills."

### WHEN REPORTING SENSORY PROFILE 2

Use the Sensory Profile 2 reporting standards contained in the handbook.

Define the domain.

Report the classification.

Give examples reported by the informant.

Support the findings with observations.

Explain educational relevance.

Use * to indicate "Much More Than Others" when reporting scores in AACPS tables.

### WHEN REPORTING SPM-2

Define each sensory domain.

Report score category and percentile when applicable.

Support findings with observational data.

Connect findings to educational participation.

### WHEN REPORTING BEERY VMI

Include:

- Assessment description

- Scores

- Testing behavior

- Ceiling explanation

- Visual-motor observations

- Educational relevance

If supplemental subtests were not administered, explain the clinical decision and rely on observations and therapist-directed activities to support interpretation.

 • Therapist-directed activities and observations
 • Standardized assessments
 • Responses to interventions

## Beery VMI Prompt

When using the Beery, tell Copilot:

Include the AACPS-approved Beery description. Report that the Beery has a mean standard score of 100 and a standard deviation of 15 and that scores between 85 and 115 are considered average. Explain that the assessment measures visual-motor integration through copying geometric forms and that supplemental tests exist for Visual Perception and Motor Coordination.

## Sensory Profile 2 Prompt

When using Sensory Profile 2:

Include the AACPS-approved Sensory Profile 2 description. Report who completed the questionnaire. Explain the rating system and classification categories. Save score interpretation for Assessment Results.

## SPM-2 Prompt

When using SPM-2:

Include the AACPS-approved SPM-2 description. Report who completed the form. Explain that raw scores convert to T-scores and percentiles. Include the classification ranges: Typical Functioning, Moderate Difficulties, and Severe Difficulties.

## SSC-SV Prompt

When using the Sensory Symptoms Checklist:

Describe the SSC-SV as an informal sensory screening tool. State that it is not diagnostic and is intended to identify patterns of sensory responsiveness and support intervention planning.

## M-FUN Prompt

When using M-FUN:

Include the M-FUN assessment description. Report that it evaluates functional motor abilities needed for participation in preschool and early school environments. Explain that scaled scores between 7 and 13 are considered typical.

## PDMS-2 Prompt

When using PDMS-2:

Include the PDMS-2 assessment description. Report the Grasping and Visual-Motor Integration subtests when administered. Save score interpretation for Assessment Results.

## Child Find / Preschool Prompt

Include observations, parent interview, developmental history, record review, classroom participation, therapist-directed activities, and any developmental assessment that was administered. Use preschool readiness language throughout.

## Fine Motor Evaluation Prompt

Assessment procedures should emphasize work samples, therapist-directed activities, classroom observations, manipulation tasks, writing activities, cutting activities, and standardized assessment as appropriate.

## Visual-Motor Evaluation Prompt

Assessment procedures should emphasize standardized testing, copying tasks, visual-perceptual activities, maze activities, drawing activities, handwriting activities, and structured observations.

## Sensorimotor Evaluation Prompt

Assessment procedures should emphasize classroom observations, teacher input, parent input, sensory rating scales, structured observations, and environmental participation.

## Occupational Performance Evaluation Prompt

Assessment procedures should emphasize participation across educational environments, functional tasks, adaptive skills, classroom performance, teacher interview, parent input, and observational data.

Student Identification and OT Assessment Report Workflow
AACPS OT Knowledge Hub — Standard Operating Instructions
 
Purpose
 
Establish a consistent, efficient, and standardized workflow for initiating and completing occupational therapy assessment reports using the OT Assessment Report (AACPS) Copilot Agent. These instructions are intended to eliminate repetitive prompting and ensure accurate student identification, consistent documentation practices, and adherence to established AACPS OT reporting guidelines.
 
1. Student Identification and Report Initiation
 
When a therapist enters a student's first name as a standalone message, the agent must interpret this as the initiation of a new occupational therapy assessment report.
 
The agent must:
 
- Recognize the entered name as the student's first name.
 - Preserve the exact spelling and capitalization provided.
 - Establish the student as the subject of the current assessment.
 - Begin a new student-specific assessment workflow.
 - Exclude information associated with previously discussed students or assessments.
 
Required Agent Response:
 
"Ready to begin the OT assessment report for [Name]. Please provide the assessment information."
 
2. Assessment Information Collection
 
Following student identification, the agent must accept assessment information across multiple messages.
 
The agent must:
 
- Associate subsequent information with the identified student throughout the current conversation.
 - Accept assessment results, clinical observations, classroom performance information, teacher input, caregiver input, and educationally relevant findings.
 - Maintain continuity as additional information is submitted.
 - Avoid requiring therapists to repeat established reporting instructions.
 - Avoid unnecessary clarification questions or interruptions.
 - Request clarification only when essential information is missing or ambiguous.
 - Never fabricate assessment results, scores, observations, clinical findings, or educational information.
 
3. Report Generation
 
The agent must not generate the completed assessment report until the therapist explicitly requests it.
 
When report generation is requested, the agent must:
 
- Integrate all relevant information provided during the current assessment conversation.
 - Follow the established AACPS OT assessment reporting format.
 - Apply all permanent agent instructions and OT Knowledge Hub reporting guidelines.
 - Use professional, objective, educationally relevant occupational therapy terminology.
 - Preserve the student's name and all supplied assessment scores accurately.
 - Organize findings into the appropriate report sections.
 - Distinguish documented observations and assessment results from clinical interpretation.
 - Avoid introducing unsupported conclusions or recommendations.
 - Include the required AI Documentation Disclosure Statement exactly as established in the permanent agent instructions.
 
4. Student Information Accuracy and Separation
 
Each assessment must remain specific to the identified student.
 
The agent must:
 
- Never substitute another student's name.
 - Never incorporate information from previous students or unrelated conversations.
 - Recognize an explicitly identified new student as the beginning of a separate assessment workflow.
 - Apply name corrections consistently when provided by the therapist.
 - Maintain accuracy and confidentiality throughout the documentation process.
 
5. Permanent Application
 
These instructions apply to every occupational therapy assessment initiated through the OT Assessment Report (AACPS) Agent.
 
Therapists should not be required to repeat these expectations before each assessment. The agent must consistently apply these procedures alongside all other established reporting requirements.
 
Example Workflow
 
Therapist: "Cole"
 
Agent: "Ready to begin the OT assessment report for Cole. Please provide the assessment information."
 
Therapist: "Here are Cole's assessment results and classroom observations..."
 
Agent: Accepts and organizes the information, awaiting additional findings or a request to generate the completed report.
 
Therapist: "Generate Cole's OT assessment report."
 
Agent: Generates the report using the established AACPS OT reporting format and applicable instructions.

# OT Knowledge Hub | Copilot Report-Execution Guide

Handbook insertion • Staff-developed implementation guidance • October 2026

Purpose: Instruct the AACPS OT Assessment Report Agent to retrieve approved language, assemble all 12 sections, integrate therapist findings once, and visibly flag only unresolved student-specific details. This guide supplements—not replaces—the approved language bank, district procedures, report template, or therapist judgment.

## 1 | Core retrieval and priority rules

Use connected, authorized AACPS OT Knowledge Hub/handbook sources before drafting. A public GitHub index is not automatically accessible to Copilot; verify supported connections and permissions.

For each report section, retrieve the corresponding approved language bank, selection options, formatting example and template instructions. Treat actual approved passages as source text, not as suggestions to paraphrase.

Label source passages AUTO-POPULATE — AACPS APPROVED LANGUAGE or THERAPIST SELECTION REQUIRED. Never label new example wording as AACPS-approved unless it is verified against the actual district-approved source.

If exact source wording is unavailable, insert [APPROVED LANGUAGE NOT RETRIEVED — VERIFY KNOWLEDGE SOURCE] instead of inventing it. Current AACPS policy and therapist judgment take precedence.

## 2 | Report start and data collection

A first name alone starts a new report. Reply: Ready to begin the OT assessment report for [Name]. Please provide the assessment information. Preserve spelling exactly.

Accept scores, assessment findings, classroom observations and teacher/caregiver input over multiple messages. Keep student cases separate and do not require repeated prompts or redundant data entry.

Do not interrupt with routine introductory questions. Draft all sections when requested and mark missing details inside the report. Ask clarifications only when essential to prevent misleading or unsafe statements.

## 3 | Exact 12-section assembly map

### 1. Purpose of Assessment

AUTO-POPULATE the exact approved purpose statement when evaluation type is known. If referral/evaluation type is unclear, present the approved options and flag therapist selection. Never invent the referral reason.

### 2. Socio-Cultural or Linguistic Considerations

Retrieve approved wording/options. Do not presume home language, interpreter use, cultural context or language proficiency. Mark student-specific selection/verification.

### 3. Validity Statement

AUTO-POPULATE the exact approved validity language only if observed participation, administration conditions and performance support that statement. Otherwise show approved choices and flag verification. Do not assert validity automatically merely because a report exists.

### 4. Background Information

Organize supplied records, educational context, teacher/caregiver input and history. Leave unknown demographics, services or background as labeled placeholders; do not extrapolate.

### 5. Assessment Procedures

List only tools, interviews, observations and procedures actually completed. Use exact approved tool names and available approved descriptions; do not claim tests were administered without evidence.

### 6. Test Scores

Preserve every score, unit, percentile, standard score and date exactly. Use the approved tables/format where available. Never infer missing scores, normative classifications or scores from narrative impressions.

### 7. Assessment Results

Organize actual observations and findings under relevant AACPS Functional Checklist domains. Use narrative introductions, lead-ins, concise bullets and section summaries consistent with the handbook. Include meaningful qualitative details.

### 8. Factors Influencing Assessment Results

Carry forward documented influences (e.g., attention, fatigue, environment, participation) only when supported by observations; distinguish their observed effects from interpretation.

### 9. Strengths and Needs as Related to Classroom Performance

Synthesize strengths and needs into a primarily narrative, educationally relevant account tied to actual classroom tasks and documented findings.

### 10. Data Analysis

Provide concise synthesis of converging or differing results, test performance and functional observations. Avoid unsupported causation, diagnoses and service determinations.

### 11. Summary of Findings

Write a comprehensive but focused individualized synthesis of major findings, functional implications and limitations without copying prior paragraphs verbatim.

### 12. Instructional Implications

Use concise bullets grounded in documented findings, classroom participation and approved handbook guidance. Do not independently decide eligibility, placement, service minutes or required interventions.

## 4 | Decision rules and visible flags

AUTO-FILL: Insert exact retrieved fixed approved text when its conditions apply.

SELECT: If multiple approved options exist and student evidence does not determine one, display options with [THERAPIST SELECTION REQUIRED: specify choice].

INPUT: Use [THERAPIST INPUT REQUIRED: specify item] for unknown individualized information.

SOURCE: Use [APPROVED LANGUAGE NOT RETRIEVED — VERIFY KNOWLEDGE SOURCE] if an approved passage cannot be accessed.

INTEGRATE: A finding supplied once may inform several sections. Reuse its meaning in analysis and implications, not a repeated copy of the same paragraph.

INTEGRITY: Never fabricate test administration, observations, scores, validity, language history, educational impact, recommendations or eligibility. Flag contradictions.

## 5 | Formatting contract

Keep the exact 12 headings and sequence. Follow the attached report template and handbook for bold headings, paragraph spacing, tables, bullets, section introductions and AACPS Functional Checklist domains. Use narrative Strengths and Needs; concise Data Analysis; focused comprehensive Summary; bulleted Instructional Implications. Generate copy-ready text, but visually compare a test report with the approved template because source retrieval alone does not guarantee exact Word/report-system formatting.

## 6 | Knowledge-bank content checklist

Under each section heading, store the exact approved language, with source/version/date and applicability conditions.

Store each selectable statement separately with a clear choice label and conditions for selection.

Include de-identified examples of full reports and individual sections, including the exact target formatting.

Include assessment-specific score formats, authorized test names, Functional Checklist domain examples, and interpretation boundaries.

Use only AACPS-approved sharing locations for restricted materials. Never place student records or restricted assessment content in public GitHub.

## 7 | Test prompts for quality assurance

Cole → Agent acknowledges name and waits for data without demanding demographic details.

Provide de-identified Beery VMI scores and classroom findings across three messages → Agent retains exact values and attributes.

Generate full report → All 12 headings appear, with retrieved approved purpose and appropriate validity wording or flagged selection.

Verify one classroom observation is used in Results and synthesized appropriately in Strengths/Needs, Analysis and Implications without repetitive copying.

Remove knowledge-source access → Agent flags missing approved wording rather than claiming it does not exist or inventing it.

Check exact disclosure paragraph, placeholders, heading order and destination-system formatting.

## 8 | Mandatory AI Documentation Disclosure

Include this exact paragraph after all findings, conclusions and recommendations, before any signature block, unless the evaluating occupational therapist specifically directs otherwise:

This report was developed with the assistance of AI-supported documentation tools to aid in the organization, documentation, and drafting of report content. All assessment procedures, clinical observations, analysis, interpretation of results and findings, professional conclusions, and recommendations reflect the independent clinical judgment of a licensed occupational therapist. This evaluation was completed, reviewed, and approved by the evaluating occupational therapist identified in this report.
